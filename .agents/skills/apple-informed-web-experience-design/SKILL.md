# Apple-Informed Web Experience Design

Design websites that help people understand an offer, trust it, and take a useful next step. Use the bundled Apple HIG Markdown as a source of transferable principles—purpose, agency, clarity, accessibility, restraint, and craft—not as a template for a native app or as authority on ecommerce conversion.

## Establish the brief

Identify audience, traffic source or entry intent, business model, offer, differentiator, desired action, brand, existing evidence, and required page type. Identify target viewports, localization, content and asset availability, and whether the deliverable is a strategy, wireframe, written copy, visual concept, critique, or implementation. Ask only for missing information that would materially change the work; otherwise state assumptions. Do not invent testimonials, numerical results, certifications, pricing, stock limits, or guarantees.

Read [references/hig-routing.md](references/hig-routing.md), then only the HIG files relevant to the task. Read [references/page-strategy.md](references/page-strategy.md) for page structure or hierarchy, [references/copy-and-conversion.md](references/copy-and-conversion.md) for selling or copywriting, and [references/disclosure-and-motion.md](references/disclosure-and-motion.md) for reveal, transition, or interactive behavior. For a full website brief, read all four.

## Make the first viewport earn attention

Treat “above the fold” as the initial visible region at a specified viewport, not a fixed pixel boundary or mandate to cram the whole pitch into one screen. Prioritize a coherent answer to: What is this? Who is it for? Why consider it? What can I do next? Use a specific offer-led headline, an explanatory supporting line, a clear primary action, and relevant imagery or product context; add compact proof or risk reduction only when substantiated. For high-intent product traffic, favor product, price, options, availability, and purchase path; for unfamiliar services, favor problem, outcome, differentiation, and contact path. Recompose for narrow/mobile viewports instead of simply shrinking desktop composition.

## Sequence the page around decisions

Let subsequent sections answer the next actual questions: benefits and mechanism, evidence, comparison or specifications, objections and practicalities, then a fitting action. Use progressive disclosure to keep advanced details accessible without hiding material terms, price, shipping, returns, limitations, or other information needed to decide. Match the amount and order of detail to intent; long pages are acceptable when scanning and navigation work. Define the primary action and any lower-commitment secondary action so they do not compete ambiguously.

## Pair copy with design

Write the actual headline, supporting copy, section leads, product/service explanations, button labels, form labels, reassurance, and meaningful empty/error states when requested. Make claims concrete and verifiable, speak in the brand's voice, and write for the customer's decision rather than an abstract “premium” aesthetic. Distinguish observed facts from proposed language or placeholders. Avoid fabricated social proof, manipulative urgency, preselected consent, hidden costs, and misleading action labels. Use [references/copy-and-conversion.md](references/copy-and-conversion.md) for page-specific prompts and examples.

## Specify reveal and transition behavior

Use motion to connect states, acknowledge input, reveal relationships, or guide focus. Specify trigger, affected element, start/end state, expected interaction, and a reduced-motion or no-motion equivalent. Keep crucial content available without animation and without requiring hover. Do not delay reading or purchase with entrance sequences or hijack scrolling. For implementation, preserve semantic controls, keyboard navigation, visible focus, accessible disclosure state, content order, performance, and responsive behavior. Read [references/disclosure-and-motion.md](references/disclosure-and-motion.md) when motion or disclosure is material.

## Deliver the requested form

- For strategy: provide audience/offer assumptions, first-viewport proposition, page narrative, conversion path, and open evidence questions.
- For design concepts or images: specify viewport, exact copy, component placement, image direction, hierarchy, and one coherent interaction state per frame; describe motion separately from a static image. Use an available image tool when an image is requested and inspect the result.
- For critique: evaluate comprehension, action clarity, proof, disclosure, accessibility, and likely friction; separate observed problems from hypotheses that need testing.
- For implementation: use the project's existing stack and relevant coding skills; test across mobile and desktop widths, keyboard and reduced-motion settings. Do not imply a static mockup proves real-world performance.

Finish with a brief check: first-viewport clarity; claim provenance; full path to action; discoverability of important details; responsive and accessible interactions; motion purpose and fallback. Propose measurement only when relevant: e.g., qualified enquiries, add-to-cart, completed purchases, or task completion alongside guardrails such as returns, support burden, and misleading clicks. Treat conversion improvement as a hypothesis until measured.
