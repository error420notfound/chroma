# Copywriting for a useful commercial decision

## Translate the offer

Collect or infer audience vocabulary, product/service facts, differentiators, objections, price/availability, promised outcome, voice, and permissible evidence. If facts are unavailable, label proposed copy as draft and leave factual claims as questions or explicit placeholders. Never produce fake testimonials, conversion numbers, endorsements, clinical claims, scarcity, or guarantees.

Draft a message ladder: plain description → user-relevant outcome → mechanism or distinguishing detail → credible evidence → proportionate call to action. Let the page answer objections in context. State costs and material limitations where they are needed for an informed choice. Prefer exact CTA verbs such as “View the menu,” “Compare plans,” or “Request a quote” to generic “Discover more” when the next step is known.

## Page-specific emphasis

- **Storefront:** Connect assortment, selection logic, and brand promise. Avoid implying every product shares the same benefit.
- **Product detail:** Explain who it suits, what is included, variants, price and total-cost factors, delivery/returns, and care or use. Ground benefits in actual features and evidence.
- **Service page:** Describe audience/problem, scope, process, typical deliverable or outcome, qualifications, and how inquiry works. Avoid promising results the business cannot control.
- **Local page:** Put offering, locality, hours/contact, booking/ordering route, and current availability where people can find them.
- **Campaign page:** Match headline and offer to the referring message, keep a focused next step, and surface terms that change the proposition.

## Edit for trust

Check whether the headline works without the image, whether the CTA predicts the next screen, whether supporting copy makes a concrete claim, and whether proof actually supports it. Replace generic claims (“crafted to perfection”) with verifiable specifics when possible. Leave room for a brand's expressive voice, but keep instructions, forms, errors, policies, and checkout unambiguous.

Choose business metrics appropriate to the journey and a quality guardrail. For example, qualified form submissions plus spam rate, or purchase completion plus refund/support rate. Do not treat higher click-through alone as proof of a better experience.
