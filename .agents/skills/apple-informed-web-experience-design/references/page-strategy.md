# Page strategy and the first viewport

## Establish the decision

Write a one-line decision statement: “For [visitor at this stage], show [offer and reason to believe] so they can [next action].” Identify the visitor's likely question, what is known versus assumed, and the level of commitment of the action. Consider entry source: branded traffic may already know the category; cold traffic needs orientation; search-to-product traffic needs product facts fast.

## Shape the initial viewport

- Lead with product or service specificity and a comprehensible benefit. A beautiful but ambiguous slogan cannot carry the whole proposition.
- Let the visual demonstrate the product, usage, result, or credible atmosphere. Specify what the image must communicate; don't rely on a generic decorative hero.
- State one primary action with a precise label; add a secondary path only if it helps an audience not ready to commit.
- Put proof, price, contact, or risk reduction in the first viewport when the visitor's decision hinges on it. Do not add badges simply to fill space.
- Account for sticky headers, mobile browser chrome, text expansion, language length, and small devices. Test what is visible at concrete viewport sizes; avoid a fixed “fold” rule.

## Match page archetype

| Archetype | Initial decision | Subsequent evidence | Typical action |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| D2C / ecommerce home | Is this relevant and worth browsing? | Categories, differentiated products, policies, real proof | Explore collection |
| Product detail | Is this the right product at an acceptable total cost? | Options, usage, specifications, delivery/returns, real reviews | Add to cart or choose option |
| Service business | Can they solve my problem and are they credible? | Process, relevant work, scope, timelines, qualifying details | Request a consultation |
| Local business | Is this what I need and can I visit/order now? | Menu/offer, location, hours, availability | View menu, get directions, book |
| Campaign landing page | Does this match the promise that brought me here? | Focused benefits, proof, objections, terms | Take campaign action |

These are starting hypotheses, not immutable templates. Preserve real differences in brand, audience, purchasing cycle, and content.

## Sequence below the viewport

Use each section to answer one adjacent question. Give scanners informative headings, short summaries, and optional deep detail. Keep navigation, search/filtering, product facts, price and terms easy to find. Reintroduce the action at meaningful decision points without repeating a full-height promotional block. If the business needs several audience paths, make the choice explicit rather than mixing conflicting pitches in one hero.

For critique, distinguish first-glance comprehension, scrolling comprehension, and task completion. For new concepts, state what should be tested rather than claiming a layout will convert.
